## 1. The short version

1. **All the round 1 gaps that can be measured in software are now closed.** That covers several settings per unit, three test frequencies (100 Hz, 1 kHz, 5 kHz), THD and IMD at 0 to 15 dB of gain reduction, frequency response at up to about 30 dB of gain reduction, attack times resolved to about 0.02 ms, bursts of 50 ms to 5 s, six kinds of music-like material at two levels, stereo link, sidechain filters, the Distressor's Dist 2 and Dist 3 modes, and IMD on the pres. The dBFS-to-dBu question is half answered: the plug-ins' Headroom knob is a pure level offset (default 16 dB), but no plug-in can tell us real volts (section 6).
2. **Glue is already very close.** On music, its gain reduction is within 0.2 to 0.4 dB rms of the SSL, and the whole output nulls 35 to 41 dB below the signal once the SSL's fixed bass roll-off is added.
3. **Natural is close on level but too slow to let go.** The static curve matches to 0.16 dB. After heavy gain reduction, though, it releases about 2.5 to 3 times slower than the LA-2A (63 % recovered at 394 ms against 138 ms). It also has none of the LA-2A's second-harmonic colour.
4. **Room Crush, Nuke and Swell are the ones that sound different.**
   - Room Crush releases 10 to 20 times slower than the 1176 (300 ms against 15 to 30 ms). On music it compresses 2 to 4 dB too much.
   - Nuke's 350 ms slow-attack branch makes it reach full gain reduction after about 300 ms, where the Distressor gets there in 2 to 3 ms. On music it under-compresses by 1 to 7 dB, worst on bass.
   - Swell's attack is 5 to 10 times slower than the Fairchild's, and it caps gain reduction at 10 dB where the Fairchild goes to 20 dB or more.
5. **Frequency response does not change with gain reduction on any unit** (all changes are under 0.2 dB at about 30 dB of gain reduction). So "dynamic EQ" convolution isn't needed.
6. **The colour that does change with compression comes from two places.** One is the gain moving inside each waveform cycle, mostly on bass (ripple). A correct detector model gives you that for free. The other is a fixed tube or transformer stage whose distortion depends on level, which Fairchild, LA-2A, Vari-Mu and FET ALL have. A small static waveshaper per preset covers it.
7. **Volterra verdict: a hybrid.** Keep the compressor model (detector and gain computer) for the movement, and add a small static colour block and the unit's fixed EQ. A Volterra-only model of a compressor failed badly in a direct test (section 4). For the pres, a low-order static waveshaper between two filters is enough; full Volterra kernels added almost nothing and higher orders over-fitted.

### 3.3 Release and bursts (`data\comp_burst_depth.csv`, `data\burst_uad_vs_models.csv`)

- **Natural (LA-2A).** Release depends on how long and how hard the unit was hit. After 1 s at 20 dB GR, the LA-2A recovers 63 % in 138 ms and 90 % in 666 ms; BLOOM takes 394 ms and 1848 ms. After light GR (5 dB for 1 to 5 s), the LA-2A is slower than BLOOM (90 % at 1.4 to 1.5 s against about 1 s). So the light-memory idea is right, but it points the wrong way after heavy hits.
- **Glue (SSL Auto).** Close: BLOOM's release is 0 to 30 % slower. The Auto release's two-stage shape matches.
- **Room Crush (1176, release 5).** The 1176 recovers 63 % in 14 to 31 ms; BLOOM takes 298 ms. This is the biggest timing difference in the study. It may be a deliberate "room" choice, so it needs your decision.
- **Nuke (Distressor).** Release is close (63 % in 106 to 128 ms against 86 to 97 ms). The problem is the attack: the 0.32-weight slow branch (350 ms attack) means BLOOM needs 270 to 325 ms to reach 90 % of GR, where the Distressor takes 2 to 3 ms.
- **Swell (Fairchild TC 5).** Release grows with burst length on the real unit (63 % in 380 ms after 50 ms, rising to 2.2 s after 5 s). BLOOM follows the trend but is too slow after medium bursts (2.2 s against 0.8 s after 1 s). It also never gets past about 9.5 dB.

### 3.5 Colour while compressing (`data\comp_colour_summary.csv`)

Each setting has a "twin": the same unit at the same input level with compression off. That separates colour caused by compressing from colour caused by level. All figures are at 1 kHz unless marked.

- **SSL G.** Almost no fixed colour (0.01–0.05 % THD, odd-order). Bass THD rises to 0.2–0.6 % at 100 Hz from gain ripple. BLOOM Glue already reproduces it (0.43 % against 0.51 % at 100 Hz), because it comes from the detector.
- **LA-2A Silver.** Jumps from 0.04 % to about 0.23 % once it compresses. H2 (second harmonic) is about −54 dBc and dominant, H3 is −57 to −60 dBc, and 100 Hz reaches 0.7–1.5 %. BLOOM Natural has no H2 at all (pure gain gives odd harmonics only) and about a third of the 100 Hz THD. **A small even-order stage is missing.** Gray is similar; LA-2 is cleaner (0.07 %).
- **1176LN 4:1.** Clean and steady at 0.06–0.13 %, H2 about −65 dBc, 100 Hz up to 0.9 % (ripple). Rev A is a little cleaner.
- **1176 ALL (Room Crush).** 0.2–0.5 % with H2 and H3 both about −50 dBc; 100 Hz 2.8–4.3 %. With the fastest attack and release it reaches 1.9–3.2 % at 1 kHz. BLOOM Room Crush gives 0.03–0.04 % at 1 kHz and 0.3 % at 100 Hz. Fixing the release brings back most of the 100 Hz part; the 1 kHz part needs a colour stage.
- **Distressor.**
  - Norm, 6:1: 0.09–0.29 %.
  - **Dist 2** adds H2: −52 to −42 dBc, 0.3–0.85 %.
  - **Dist 3** adds H3: −47 to −42 dBc, 0.46–0.85 %.
  - At 1:1 with the input knob at 5, the stage saturates hard near full scale (16–40 % THD).
  - On IMD, Dist 2 and Dist 3 raise CCIF by about 10–15 dB over Norm.
- **Fairchild.** 0.04 % at 3 dB GR, rising to 1.2 % at 15 dB, H3-dominant (−39 dBc). The twin shows nearly the same (0.03–0.99 %). **So Fairchild colour is level-driven (tube stage), not GR-driven.**
- **Vari-Mu.** 0.05–0.17 % at 1 kHz but 1.1 % at 5 kHz, with a very strong even-order CCIF difference tone (−36 to −15 dBc). Its high end gets dirty when driven. The "hot" setting reaches 1.6 %.
- **LA-3A.** Odd-only (H2 at −145 dBc), 0.18–0.36 %; 100 Hz 1.7–3.1 % (ripple).
- **dbx 160.** H3 at a steady −67 dBc; 100 Hz 0.45 % (fast RMS detector ripple).

### 3.6 Frequency response (`data\comp_fr_vs_gr.csv`)

There's no change with gain reduction on any unit (every band moves less than 0.2 dB between 0 and about 30 dB GR). The fixed responses:
- Bass at 20 Hz: about −3.4 dB on most units, −4.5 dB on the 1176s, −5.5 dB on the Vari-Mu, −6.8 dB on the Fairchild.
- Top: the LA-2A lifts the top by +0.6 dB at 15 kHz (Gray +0.9 dB).
- Every plug-in falls to about −42 dB at 20 kHz. That's the plug-in's oversampling filter, not hardware, so **don't copy it**.

### 3.7 Sidechain and stereo (`data\comp_sidechain.csv`, `data\comp_link.csv`)

**Sidechain** (GR from a tone at each frequency, at a level giving 10 dB GR at 1 kHz):
- SSL filter 100 and 250 are high-pass filters on the detector.
- Fairchild 100 and 250 are similar.
- The Distressor's **HP** is about a 100 Hz high-pass. **Emp** is an HF-weighting of about +4.5 dB at 5–8 kHz.
- The 1176 and dbx "sidechain filter" options are a steady treble tilt, not a high-pass (2 dB of GR at 30 Hz rising to 25 dB at 16 kHz).
- **LA-2A emphasis** (R37): at 100, it makes the unit least sensitive around 300–500 Hz and more sensitive at both ends.
- BLOOM Nuke's 1st-order 100 Hz detector HPF is close to the Distressor's HP.

**Stereo:** every unit is linked by default, so R gets the same GR as L, except two:
- Distressor **Norm is unlinked**. Its **Link** mode averages the two channels: L gets only 4.6 dB where it would get 10 dB alone.
- Fairchild and Vari-Mu unlink properly when switched.
- Crosstalk is negligible except FET ALL (−65 dBc) and the Fairchild Lat/Vert matrix (−75 dBc).

## 4. Volterra / dynamic convolution: the test and the verdict

**The test.** For each BLOOM reference unit I fitted the Volterra-type model a "nonlinear convolution" plug-in would use: a generalised Hammerstein, orders 1 and 5. I fitted it to the unit's own sweeps at four levels (−30, −18, −6, +6 dBFS), then asked it to predict the music. Null depth (lower is better):

| Unit | Volterra-only (best of orders 1, 5), base / +8 dB | BLOOM current + UAD EQ, base / +8 dB |
|---|---|---|
| SSL (Glue) | −16 to −23 / −10 to −17 | −37 to −41 / −38 to −41 |
| LA-2A (Natural) | −10 to −17 / −6 to −13 | −28 to −32 / −23 to −28 |
| 1176 ALL (Room Crush) | −10 to −14 / −4 to −8 | −16 to −21 / −13 to −18 |
| Fairchild (Swell) | −12 to −19 / −6 to −15 | −21 to −30 / −14 to −22 |
| Distressor (Nuke) | −5 to −10 / −4 to −9 | −14 to −18 / −10 to −16 |

(`data\vt_comp.csv`; BLOOM figures from `data\prog_vs_models.csv`, same programs.)

**Why it loses.** A Volterra kernel set describes a system whose behaviour depends only on the last few milliseconds of signal. A compressor's gain depends on the last 50 ms to 5 s, through a detector with separate attack and release paths. The kernels would need seconds of memory and huge cross-terms. Fitted from sweeps, they learn "the average gain at the level of the sweep", and that is wrong for music, more so when it's hot. Switching kernels by level ("dynamic convolution", as in Nebula-style plug-ins) only helps when the unit's EQ changes with level. Section 3.6 shows it doesn't, on any of these units.

**Verdict: a hybrid, for every compressor.**

1. **Detector and gain computer** (BLOOM's existing model). This sets the movement, and with it the ripple distortion on bass. It is where most of the remaining error is (Room Crush, Nuke, Swell).
2. **Static colour block.** A short memoryless waveshaper (low-order polynomial or tanh mix, with H2/H3 weights fitted from the THD-vs-level tables). Put it before the gain stage for tube or transformer input colour (Fairchild, Vari-Mu, Distressor Dist modes). Put it after for output-stage colour (LA-2A H2, FET ALL). No kernels needed.
3. **Fixed EQ.** Two or three biquads: the bass roll-off, plus the LA-2A's top lift.

**For the pres: a Wiener–Hammerstein model** (filter → static waveshaper of order 3 or lower → filter). Add a bass pre-emphasis into the waveshaper for the 610-A and Manley, and a 20–50 ms level sag for the Manley only. Full Volterra isn't worth its CPU cost: higher orders over-fitted on 3 of the 6 pres.

## 6. dBFS ↔ dBu

On the Fairchild and SSL G, changing Headroom from the default 16 dB to 8 dB moves the whole static curve by 8 dB and leaves THD-vs-GR unchanged. So the knob is a pure level offset. UAD's documented convention is that 0 VU (+4 dBu) sits at −Headroom dBFS. That puts **−16 dBFS = +4 dBu, so 0 dBFS = +20 dBu**, on the units that have the knob. This is inferred from the knob and the convention, not measured in volts. The other units (LA-2A, 1176, Distressor, dbx, Vari-Mu, LA-3A, pres) have no Headroom knob, so for those the only usable reference is still where they compress or clip (the 1 %-THD points from round 1).
